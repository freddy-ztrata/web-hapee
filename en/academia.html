Connected channels

03

WhatsApp

Where: menu Conversations → WhatsApp

The channel where Latin America buys. Hapee connects to the official WhatsApp Business API: unlimited messages per conversation, no phone that has to stay on, and no risk of being banned for using unofficial apps.

Before you start
A number for WhatsApp Business (can be a new one)Admin access to your Facebook/Meta page
Steps
  1. Go to Conversations → WhatsApp and follow the connection wizard with your Meta account (it will ask you to log in to Facebook and authorize the number).
  2. Verify the number with the SMS code Meta sends.
  3. Complete the business profile: photo, description and hours — it's what customers see when they open your chat.
  4. Send yourself a test message from another phone and confirm it shows up in the Inbox.
The rule you need to understand: the 24-hour window

Official WhatsApp works on a simple rule: when a customer writes to you, a 24-hour window opens in which you can reply with whatever you want. After those 24 hours —or if YOU want to write first— you can only start with a template approved by Meta.

SituationWhat you can send
Customer wrote < 24 h agoAny free-form message: text, photos, audio, documents. This is where your agents operate.
Customer wrote > 24 h agoOnly approved templates (the conversation "reopens" if they reply).
You start the conversationOnly approved templates. Workflows that send "cold" WhatsApp messages need them.

Create your 3 base templates from day one and submit them for approval (it takes hours, sometimes a day). Templates can't be aggressively promotional — Meta rejects them; write them as customer service. You can start from these:

# 1 · Welcome
Hi {{nombre}}, thanks for reaching out to [your company] 👋
Tell us how we can help and we'll get back to you right away.

# 2 · Appointment reminder
Hi {{nombre}}, a reminder of your appointment on {{fecha}} at {{hora}}
with [your company]. If you need to reschedule, just reply here.

# 3 · Quote follow-up
Hi {{nombre}}, were you able to review the quote we sent you?
We're here for any questions — and if you'd like, we can go over it together
on a quick call.
Done when… you message the number from your personal phone, the message appears in the Hapee inbox, and your 3 base templates have been submitted for approval.
Heads up: if your number is already on the regular WhatsApp app, you need to release it first (delete the account in the app). If this step feels tricky, this is exactly the moment to message us: we'll do it with you on a short call.

Deals pipeline

Where: menu CRM → Deals · Pipeline

The sales kanban: each column a stage, each card a deal with its amount.

Your sales in columns: from "new lead" to "closed." Seeing a full pipeline means seeing how much money is in play and where it's getting stuck.

Steps
  1. Define your stages using the names of YOUR real process. Rule of thumb: between 4 and 6 stages; more than that and nobody moves cards.
  2. Put an amount on every deal when you create it, even an estimate: a pipeline without money is just a pretty list.
  3. Create your first 5 real deals — the ones you currently have in your head or in a notebook.
  4. Agree on a weekly ritual with the team: one pass through the pipeline, moving cards and noting the next step for each.
Done when… the pipeline shows your real sales in progress and the total amount they add up to.

08

Hapee Agents · your AI team

Where: menu Hapee Agents · control panel in Agents dashboard

A Hapee agent isn't a chatbot that recites answers: it's a digital employee with a role, personality, knowledge base, tools and limits. It replies on your channels, qualifies leads, books on your real calendar and logs everything in the CRM.

How to think about an agent: the 6 pieces
PieceWhat it isExample
RoleThe position it holds. Defines what it does and doesn't do."SDR" (qualifies and books) · "Support" (resolves questions) · "Closer" (pushes the close)
PersonaVisible name, tone and traits. It's who your customer thinks they're talking to."Valentina, sales rep, friendly, informal, uses emojis sparingly"
GoalThe measurable aim of each conversation, with its success criterion."Get name + neighborhood + need and book a visit" → success = appointment created
KnowledgeThe document bases it can talk about. It can have several."Sales" base: prices and plans · "After-sales" base: warranties and returns
ToolsWhat it can DO, not just say. Each one is turned on separately.Book on your calendar · create the deal in the pipeline · label the contact
GuardrailsThe hard limits: what it's forbidden to do, with a fallback message."Don't promise discounts" · "Don't give legal/medical advice" · "Don't make up stock"
The 3 modes — and which to use when
ModeWhat it doesUse it when…
OffDoesn't reply to anything.You're still loading knowledge.
SuggestionWrites the reply but does NOT send it: your team sees it and fixes or approves it from the inbox.The first week, always. It's your test run with real customers and zero risk.
AutopilotReplies and acts on its own.When, in suggestion mode, you've been approving its replies with almost no edits for several days.
The right path is always the same: off → suggestion (1 week) → autopilot. Skipping the suggestion stage is the #1 cause of agents being turned off within a week.
The primary agent

You can have several agents, but one is the primary: the one that automatically handles every incoming message that doesn't have another agent assigned. Mark your sales agent as primary — it's the one that receives strangers. The others (support, after-sales) are assigned to their specific channels or conversations.

The same four sections work for any industry: change the who, the what, the goal and the rules. A clinic hands off "diagnosis topics" to the doctor; an e-commerce store hands off "exchanges and returns past the deadline."

Setup steps
  1. Create the agent in Hapee Agents: visible name, SDR/sales role, chat type.
  2. Paste your instructions, adapted from the template above.
  3. Create the "Sales" knowledge base and upload: price list, description of each product/service, coverage, hours, payment methods and the answers to your 10 most frequent questions. Short, specific documents work better than one giant PDF.
  4. Turn on its tools: book on calendar (choose which), create deal in the pipeline and label contact. If any of them makes you nervous, mark it requires approval: the agent will ask permission before running it.
  5. Turn on the guardrails from the library that apply (don't promise discounts, no legal/medical advice, no third-party personal data) and write your own if your industry needs it.
  6. Adjust the fine-tuning with the table above.
  7. Set it to suggestion mode and mark it as primary.
  8. Test it with the checklist below before even thinking about autopilot.

After takeoff: the dashboard

The Agents dashboard shows every run: what it replied, which tools it used and where it got stuck. Review it 10 minutes a week for the first 4 weeks: every weak conversation tells you exactly which knowledge document is missing. Heads up: if the agent replies vaguely, 95% of the time it isn't the AI: that data just isn't in its knowledge. Before touching the instructions, check which document is missing.

09

Mail Marketing

Where: menu Marketing → Mail Marketing

Campaigns and automatic emails to your list, with open and click metrics, and everything logged on each contact's record.

Before you start
Access to your domain's control panel (or to whoever manages it)
Steps
  1. Set up your sender: the module shows you 2–3 records to add to your domain (called SPF and DKIM). Put simply, they're the ID card of your emails — they prove to Gmail and Outlook that you sent that email and not an impersonator. Without them, your campaigns land in spam even if the content is perfect. You add them ONCE in the panel where you bought your domain (NIC, GoDaddy, Cloudflare…): copy each record as is and paste it as a TXT record. If nobody on your team manages the domain, message us and we'll sort it out with your provider in a 15-minute call.
  2. Once the domain verifies, send a test campaign to your own email and check that it arrives with your name and your logo.
  3. Create your first real campaign with one simple rule: a single goal per email (an offer, a piece of news, a reminder — not all three).
  4. After 48 hours, look at opens and clicks in the same module.
Done when… your test campaign lands in your inbox (not in spam) with your sender name.
10

Forms

Where: menu Marketing → Forms

Each form creates the contact in the CRM instantly, with a tracked source: you'll know which campaign or page each lead came from.

Steps
  1. Create a form with the minimum fields: name, phone and what they need. Every extra field you ask for costs you leads — the rule of thumb: if a field doesn't change your first reply, it doesn't belong on the form; the agent asks about it later.
  2. Take advantage of the source: each form records where the submission came from, so create one form per use ("Website quote," "April campaign landing," "Trade show") instead of one generic form — reporting will later tell you which brings the best customers.
  3. Embed it on your website (the module gives you the code or the direct link — the link also works for your Instagram bio and QR codes).
  4. Connect its welcome workflow (recipe 1 in module 13): a form with no automatic reply is a customer waiting.
  5. Send a test response and confirm the contact appears in the CRM.
Done when… filling in the form creates a new contact in the CRM with its source visible.
11

Social media

Where: menu Marketing → Social media

You plan the month's content grid, schedule the posts and Hapee publishes on its own to your accounts, with a visual calendar of what's coming.

Steps
  1. Connect your accounts (Instagram and Facebook with your Meta login; any other networks you use, with their own authorization).
  2. Build the grid with the weekly 3-2-1 rule: 3 value pieces (tips, data, education about your industry), 2 social-proof pieces (cases, testimonials, behind the scenes) and 1 commercial piece (an offer or direct call to action). Accounts that only post offers stop being seen — the algorithm and people punish them alike.
  3. Schedule your first 3 posts at different times. A reference for Latin America: 8–9 AM, 1–2 PM and 8–10 PM concentrate attention; then compare with your own data in the module.
  4. Check that they publish on their own and show up as "published" in the calendar.
  5. Set the ritual: one monthly 1-hour session gets the month's grid scheduled. Posting "whenever we can" is the silent death of a small business's social media.
Done when… a scheduled post goes out on its own at the set time, without anyone touching a phone.

Workflows · real automation

Where: menu Automation → Workflows · visual node editor

A workflow is a living rule: when something happens (the trigger), Hapee does something (the actions), optionally with waits and conditions in between. You build them by dragging nodes onto a visual canvas — no code.

Anatomy of a workflow

Trigger → (wait) → (condition) → action → action… A contact "enters" the workflow when its trigger occurs, and moves forward node by node. In each workflow you can see how many contacts are inside and what step they're on.

Steps for your first workflow
  1. Start with recipe 1 (Welcome): it's the shortest and the one that shows the most.
  2. Create it in Workflows, choose the Form submitted trigger and your form.
  3. Add the Send WhatsApp action and write the message with {{nombre}}. Read it out loud: if it sounds like a machine, rewrite it.
  4. Add Assign owner (round-robin if there are several) and Create deal.
  5. Turn it on and test it yourself: fill in the form with your details and time it. You should get the WhatsApp in under a minute and see the deal created.
  6. Only once recipe 1 has run cleanly for a week, build 2 and 3.
The golden rules of automation
RuleWhy
1 workflow at a timeThree half-tested automations create more noise than none. Turn one on, test it for a week, and only then the next one.

A bot of our own joins your video calls (Meet, Zoom, Teams), truly listens to the audio, and when it's over hands you a summary, transcript, tasks with an owner and due date, and a draft follow-up email. In sales meetings it adds a sentiment meter and insights on your presentation.
